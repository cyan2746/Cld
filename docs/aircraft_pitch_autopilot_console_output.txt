  FUNDAMENTAL LIMIT (drives the whole comparison):
    plant zero time constant b1/b0 = 6.487 s
    => integral of the tracking error over a step response obeys
       int(theta_cmd - theta)dt = 6.487 - sum(1/|closed-loop pole|)  [x command]
    * no integrator in the controller (P, LQR): a slow pole is unavoidable
      -> monotonic but slow approach to the target
    * integrator in the controller (PID, LQI): the identity gives exactly 0
      -> fast rise paid for with a small overshoot, no long tail

  GUST AUTHORITY (why every design lets the upset through):
    the specified gust imposes 6.00 deg/s of pitch rate (3.0 deg in 0.50 s)
    full elevator can generate 4.81 deg/s nose-up / 2.89 deg/s nose-down,
    and needs ~3.1 s (servo lag + pitch-rate build-up) to get there.
    => the upset CANNOT be opposed while it happens; 3.0 deg of attitude
       excursion is unavoidable and the designs differ only in how fast
       and how smoothly they RECOVER.

   * hand-placed theta-only poles are unusable here: fast ones need |L| in the
     hundreds, amplify sensor noise, and throw 20-64 deg of elevator at a 3.0 deg gust.
   * inside the Kalman family a LAZY filter (small qw) flatters itself: it barely
     reacts to the sensor, so margins and gust elevator look excellent -- and then
     the last column shows what happens when the airframe is not the one it
     believes in.  Estimator bandwidth is exactly what corrects model error.
   * so the rule is: take the FASTEST filter that still meets PM >= 35 deg,
     |S|inf <= 2.50 and a gust elevator demand <= 15.0 deg.  Relax cfg.spec.pm_min
     if you would rather have the gust rejection than the robustness.

  VERDICT
    LQI is the best-implemented design for this airframe.
    * LQI settles the 10.0 deg command in 3.71 s versus 17.59 s (PID) and 29.80 s (LQR).
    * peak elevator demand 9.84 deg of the +25/-15 deg available (39% of authority).
    * gust upset of +3.0 deg is removed in 13.42 s.

    WHY, in terms of Section 2:
    * LQR has no integrator, so the identity int(err)dt = 6.49 - sum(1/|p|) forces
      a closed-loop pole slower than the plant zero (-0.154).  Its step response is
      beautifully monotonic (0.0% overshoot) but drags a 30 s tail, and it leaves
      4.237 deg of standing error under a mis-trim because nothing integrates.
      Analytically that bias should cost only -1.193 deg (= bias/K_theta) with the
      full state, but -4.239 deg through the observer: the estimator cannot see
      the bias, so the large pitch-rate gain amplifies its own estimation error.
    * PID adds the integrator, so the tail becomes a small overshoot instead, and
      the mis-trim error goes to zero.  But it only sees theta: the rate term is a
      filtered derivative of the measurement, so damping costs elevator (peak 16.8 deg).
    * LQI has both the integrator AND full-state feedback (alpha and q from the
      observer), so it damps the short period without waiting for a derivative and
      needs the least elevator (9.8 deg peak) while being the fastest.
    * Cost of that performance: LQI needs an observer (Section 8) and therefore a
      trustworthy model; PID needs one sensor and no model at all.
